Forms, messages and alerts

The site forms post to Markstage on your own host: spam checks, limits, team mail and thank-you mail, the Inbox for messages and who hears about which alerts.

Part of the API contract (docs/API.md: its conventions, prefix and errors apply here). Split out of it to keep each file readable: who hears about what (the alert rules and each person's choices), the site's forms, and the Inbox.

Alerts: who hears about what

The site's rules (Settings → Alerts) say per kind whether it is on, which roles and people it reaches (besides the people it is about, such as who started the job), extra addresses, and the channels it leaves by (email, slack, webhook); the editor always shows it. Each person then chooses per kind that can reach them (My alerts): on or off, and now (mail at once), digest (one mail a day at notify.digestHour, server time) or editor (only in the editor). A person's default is now when their role is in notify.email.roles (who got alert mail before there were rules), else editor. Safety kinds (backup.failed, user.invited, user.role, env.rules-changed, signin.new-device) are always on and always go by mail; a person chooses only now or digest. A rule's extra addresses default to notify.email.to, except for a personal kind (a sign-in, a message given to you). Mail comes from the rules' sender name (else the name in notify.email.from) at notify.email.from's address; the subject keeps the site's name.

  • GET site/alerts (editor and above) → {version, digestHour, channels: {email: {on, from, senderName, host, status, detail, lastAt}, slack: {on, host, status, detail, lastAt}, webhook: {…}}, kinds: [{kind, label, group, safety, on, roles, users: [{id, name}], extra, channels}], people: [{id, name, role}]}. A channel not set up is {on: false}; status is how its last send went (ok, error or null), detail its answer.
  • PUT site/alerts {version, senderName?, kinds: [{kind, on?, roles?, userIds?, extra?, channels?}]} (owner, administrator): only the kinds and keys sent change. 409 stale with current when version is stale; 400 in plain words for an unknown kind or role, a person who is not a user, more than 20 addresses or one that is not an address, a sender name with < > @ " , ; :, or a safety kind turned off or out of mail. Kept in <dataDir>/alert-rules.json (0600; addresses never go in the repo). Audited site.alerts {changed: [{kind, from, to}], senderName?: {from, to}} → the view plus changed: [kinds, 'senderName'].
  • GET me/alerts (anyone signed in) → {version, digestHour, kinds: [{kind, label, group, on, delivery, locked}]}, the kinds that can reach this person (by role, by name, or about them); PUT me/alerts {version?, kinds: [{kind, on, delivery}]} → the view plus changed (409 stale; 400 for a safety kind off or editor; off keeps the delivery). Audited me.alerts {changes: [{kind, from, to}]} (to is a delivery or off).

POST site/notify/test {channel?} (owner, administrator) sends a test alert through that channel (or every one): {channels: [{channel: "email"|"slack"|"webhook", ok, detail}]} (detail: "sent to …", "answered 200", or what went wrong in plain words). 400 for an unknown channel; 409 not-set-up without notify or that channel. Audited (notify.test). GET site/health says which are set up: alerts: {email, slack, webhook}.

Forms and the Inbox

The site's forms post to Markstage on the site's own host names (config.json siteHosts), never on the CMS host; there the rest of Markstage answers 404. Each form is described in the site's markstage.config forms (or config.json forms; docs/CONFIG.md): its fields, the visitor context kept beside them, the spam checks, the daily limit and the mail. Messages are kept in the data dir, never in the content repo.

POST <basePath>/forms/<id> (site host; form-encoded or JSON, at most 64 KB):

  • Origin must be https:// one of siteHosts (403 otherwise, audited security.origin).
  • Limits: at most perDay (3) messages a day from one address or for one email address, any case (429 {status: 429, error: limitMessage}). The address is the connection's, or the hop a trusted proxy appended (trustProxy); a posted ip is ignored. Spam counts against a cap of 20 a day from one address and 60 a day from one network (an IPv4 /24, an IPv6 /64).
  • A flood: past perHour (300) real messages (spam never counts) in an hour, the form is not turned off: a message is stored with status held, the visitor is answered as usual, nobody is mailed and nothing goes to the CRM yet, and the administrators get a form.held alert (once an hour per form, audited forms.held). Every minute, held messages go out oldest first while the hour is under the cap again (status new, the team mail, the thank-you, the CRM and the lead as for any message, "let through" in the history). Nobody can set held by hand; a person may still mark a held message handled or spam.
  • Spam: a filled-in honeypot field, or a started time (ms) less than minSeconds (3) ago: kept with status spam and answered like a real message (200), so a bot learns nothing; nobody is told or mailed.
  • Fields: only the form's own fields are kept (anything else posted is dropped). Text loses control characters, one-line fields lose line breaks; required, min, max, type (email, tel: 7 to 15 digits, url: http(s)) and options are checked: 400 {status: 400, error, fields: {key: message}}.
  • Then: the message is stored, and only then 200 {status: 200, message} (a page with no script, Sec-Fetch-Mode: navigate, gets 303 to thanks): the answer never waits on mail. Editors and above get a form.message notice, and the alert channels carry it: the email is the team mail (every field and where the visitor came from, Reply-To the visitor, subject team.subject). With reply (or one set in Settings → Forms, below), the visitor gets the thank-you mail through notify.email, from the alerts' sender name (greeted by name unless the name looks like a link or an address). A team mail that fails is kept on the message (alert: 'failed', the reason, its history) and tried again after 5, 15, 60, 240 and 720 minutes, then daily, 12 tries in all; Site health counts them (forms: {alertFailed}).
  • Place: with a city database on the server (docs/DEPLOY.md "Forms"), meta.location is the approximate place of the visitor's address ("Toronto, Ontario, Canada"), looked up when the message comes in; never what was posted. Its parts (city, region, country code) are kept beside it for a CRM's own fields (not in the API).
  • CRM: a form with crm (docs/CONFIG.md) also sends each message to Odoo as a lead, after the answer (below, "Odoo"). Spam never goes, and nor does anything outside production.
  • Retention (hourly): messages are kept forever unless the form sets keepDays; spam goes after 30 days, and the visitor's address and browser are cleared after 30 days (forms.retention in the audit with the counts).

The Inbox: every message from the site's forms and the viewer's own alerts in one list (editor and above; owner and administrator also delete, export and see addresses). A message is new, handled, spam or held (by the server, during a flood), can be given to a person, and gets notes for the team; an alert is new until seen (handled).

  • GET inbox?q=&kind=message|alert&status=new|handled|spam|held&form=&service=&assignee=<user id>|none&alert=failed&crm=failed&from=&to=&page= → {items, total, page, pages, perPage: 50, facets: {forms: [{id, label, count}], services: [{value, count}], status: {new, handled, spam, held}, alertFailed, crmFailed}, credits}, newest first. credits: the line the city database asks to show with its places (DB-IP's: "IP Geolocation by DB-IP (https://db-ip.com), CC BY 4.0"), or null. A message item: {id, kind: 'message', at, status, form, formLabel, from: {name, email}, service, summary, assignee: {id, name} | null, imported: {from: 'wordpress', id} | null, alert: null | 'pending' | 'sent' | 'none' | 'failed', crm: null | {state: 'pending' | 'sent' | 'failed', name: 'Odoo', leadId, error}} (error: why, when failed); an alert: {id, kind: 'alert', at, status, alertKind, level, link, summary, assignee: null}. Spam is left out unless asked for; form, service, assignee, alert, crm, status=spam and status=held list messages only. q finds text in the name, email, fields and context, and in an alert's words (% and _ are literal). from and to are days (2026-10-28, UTC). 400 in words for anything else. The form.message alerts are the messages themselves, so they are not listed twice.
  • GET inbox/:id → the item plus fields: [{key, label, type, value}] (in the form's order; a field the form no longer has under its own name), meta: {page, referrer, landing, referringDomain, utmSource, utmMedium, utmCampaign, location} (owner and administrator also get ip and userAgent), spam, alertTries, alertError, alertNextAt, crmTries, crmError, crmNextAt, crmAt, notes: [{id, at, by: {id, name}, text}], history: [{at, by: {id, name} | null, change}] and version (r<n>; an alert: seen or unseen). 404 for another person's alert.
  • PATCH inbox/:id {status?, assigneeId?, version}: 409 stale with current when someone changed it meanwhile. assigneeId is an active editor, administrator or owner (or null); they get a form.assigned alert. Each change goes in the history ("marked it handled", "gave it to Sam") and is audited inbox.change {form, status?: {from, to}, assignee?: {from, to}}, never the message text. On an alert only status (seen or not); giving it or spam is 400.
  • POST inbox/:id/notes {text} (at most 5000 characters) → 201 {id, at, by, text}; audited inbox.note.
  • POST inbox/:id/crm ("Send to Odoo again"; editor and above): one try now → the message, or 502 crm-failed {message: "Not sent: …", item}. 409 sent when it is in Odoo already, 409 not-for-crm for a message its form did not send (or that came from WordPress: old leads are never sent again). Audited inbox.crm {form, lead} with ok.
  • DELETE inbox/:id (owner, administrator; with its notes and history): audited inbox.delete {form, at, status}.
  • GET inbox.csv?…the same filters (owner, administrator): every matching message (inbox.csv), columns reference, form, at, status, assigned to, one per field, the context values, notes, imported from, imported id; a cell starting with =, +, - or @ is made text. Audited inbox.export with the count.

The thank-you mail (Settings → Forms; owner, administrator): the site's forms.<id>.reply gives the first words; a change here is kept in <dataDir>/forms-settings.json (0600) and wins until it is put back. GET site/forms → {version, siteName, variables, forms: [{id, label, canReply, reply: {subject, text} | null, fromConfig, changed}]}; PUT site/forms/:id/reply {version, on, subject, text} (on: false: none) and DELETE site/forms/:id/reply {version} (back to the site's own): 409 stale, 404 for no such form, 400 in words; audited forms.reply.

Old WordPress contact messages come in with markstage-server forms import-wordpress (docs/DEPLOY.md "Forms"). They are never sent to Odoo: WordPress kept no record of which went (its wp_contact table has no such column).

Odoo

A form with crm: {kind: 'odoo', source, …} (docs/CONFIG.md "Forms") sends each message to Odoo as a lead, the way the old WordPress theme did (functions.php odooInsert), over XML-RPC (src/forms/crm.mjs, xmlrpc.mjs):

  1. /xmlrpc/2/common authenticate(db, email, password, {}) → the user id.
  2. /xmlrpc/2/object execute_kw search on utm.source (name = crm.source), res.country (code = the visitor's country code) and res.country.state (name = the visitor's region): the first id, or false. They are looked up, never created, as before. No place: no country or state search.
  3. crm.lead create with the old theme's keys and values:
Odoo fieldValue
name, contact_namethe form's name field
email_fromthe form's email field
phonecrm.phone (default the phone or number field)
mobile_country_codethe visitor's country code from their address (the city file), in capitals, e.g. CA; nothing found: empty
descriptioncrm.description (default the first long text): tags out, on one line, HTML-escaped (WordPress's sanitize_text_field then esc_html)
country_id, state_id, source_idthe ids found above, or false
citythe visitor's city from the city database (empty without one)
url, referred, landing_url, refering_domainmeta.page, meta.referrer, meta.landing, meta.referringDomain
is_auto_createdtrue

Left out on purpose: the file attachment (the old handler attached a server file named in the request) and its res.users search (its answer was never used). Ids go as numbers and a missing one as false; the old code sent the source id as text and a missing id as 0 or "", which Odoo reads the same way.

Leads for analytics (Skald)

A form with leads: {kind: 'skald', url, …} (docs/CONFIG.md "Forms") also tells an analytics tool about each real message, as a lead, so the tool can count it and join it to the visit (src/forms/leads.mjs). Skald only for now, signed exactly as Skald's own skald-lead.cjs signs it:

  • POST <leads.url> (Skald's lead hook, https://app.skaldanalytics.com/traffic-hooks/<site>/lead), JSON, with X-Skald-Signature: t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex HMAC-SHA256(secret, "<t>.<body>")>.
  • The lead: external_ref (the message's id, so a repeat is never counted twice), action (leads.action, default contact), email and name (the form's fields), ip (the visitor's address as Markstage recorded it: the connection, or the hop a trusted proxy appended; Skald looks up the country and drops it), vid (the visitor id the page posted in leads.vid, Skald's skald('id'), when it is one), test: true (when the page posted 1 in leads.test: a team's own test, which Skald keeps out of its counts).

Spam (the honeypot, a form sent too fast) never goes, and nothing goes outside production. It runs after the message is stored and the visitor answered; a send that fails is tried again like the Odoo push (5, 15, 60, 240, 720 minutes, then daily; given up after 12 tries). Each outcome is in the message's history ("sent to Skald as a lead", "not sent to Skald as a lead: …"), GET /inbox/:mid has lead (how it went), and Site health counts the ones that did not go. The secret is read from the environment at each send (SKALD_LEAD_SECRET; leads.env picks another prefix) and is never written, logged or audited. test/odoo.test.mjs reads the keys out of a verbatim copy of the theme's $leadData array (test/odoo-lead-data.php.txt) and checks that the lead the stand-in Odoo gets has those keys and no others, so a field left out or renamed fails it.

When: after the message is stored and the visitor answered, never in the way of it. It is marked pending, then sent with the lead's id (crm.leadId; history "sent to Odoo (lead 123)"), or failed with the reason in words (crmError), tried again after 5, 15, 60, 240 and 720 minutes, then daily, 12 tries in all (history "not sent to Odoo: …", then "gave up…"); "Send to Odoo again" tries at once. Site health: forms.crm: {forms: [{form, label, kind, name, on, problem}], failed}. In the editor's Inbox: a "Not sent to Odoo" mark on the message (why on hover or focus, with a copy icon), the filter #/inbox?crm=failed with its count ("2 messages not sent to Odoo"), and in the message's drawer (&open=<id>) its tries, when it is tried next, why, and "Send to Odoo again", which shows the server's words when it is refused (502 "Not sent: …", 409 "It is in Odoo already (lead N)."). Proof: e2e/inbox-odoo.e2e.mjs.

The connection is read from the environment at each push, never from a file in the repo: <env>_URL (https; http only on this machine; no sign-in in it), <env>_DB, <env>_EMAIL, <env>_PASSWORD, and <env>_ENABLE = true (env defaults to ODOO: the old theme's names). Without _ENABLE=true nothing is marked or sent, as before; anything else missing fails in words that name the variable. The values are never logged, audited, kept or answered: an error that quotes one has it replaced by [hidden].
