Which is the best CMS for Astro? Markstage compared

The best CMS for Astro depends on your team: who edits, where content lives and what you want to run. Here is where Markstage, a visual editor that keeps content in git, fits next to WordPress, Ghost, Prismic and other hosted and git-based CMSs, and where each one leads. Markstage is not affiliated with any of them.

The short verdict

  • Pick WordPress

    if you want its very large ecosystem of themes, plugins, hosts and people who know it, or a familiar admin for content that Astro reads by API.

  • Pick Ghost

    if the site is a publication: newsletters, memberships and paid subscriptions are built in 97, with its own editor and themes, self-hosted or on Ghost(Pro). Its code is under the MIT licence 95.

  • Pick Prismic

    if you want a hosted page builder with nothing to run, and your developers build the sections ("slices") 71.

  • Pick Markstage

    if writers should edit on the Astro site's own design, content should stay as files in git, and staging, roles and single sign-on should come included on your own server. Markstage is pre-release; WordPress and Ghost have many years of production use and much larger communities.

Markstage is pre-release. The marks above are what it does today; a badge beside a mark says a change is on the way. Planned, later

    Install @markstage/astro from npm, without a clone of the repository.

  • The real page on the server, too Planned

    The built editor on a server draws each article inside its real page, as astro dev does today.

  • More than one server process Planned

    Sign-in and share-link limits shared between processes, so one data dir can run on several.

  • Adapters beyond Astro Planned

    The core has no framework in it; more site generators after Astro.

  • Live co-editing Planned

    Several people in one page at once, with live cursors, beside today's locks and three-way merge.

  • AI translation of a whole site Planned

    Draft every page of a site in another language in one run, for people to review.

  • A stable 1.0 Planned

    After deeper quality, security and performance work.

How it fits

WordPress, Ghost and Prismic come first: they are the three people weigh most often. Facts about other products come from each vendor's own website, pricing page, docs or code repository, checked on 2026-11-03 (Ghost's on 2026-11-04). The full matrix, with a source for every cell and 13 more products (Sveltia CMS, Pages CMS, Front Matter CMS, Outstatic, Sitepins, EmDash, StudioCMS, Sanity, Contentful, Builder.io, Payload, Strapi and Statamic), is in docs/COMPARISON.md.

WordPress and Ghost are mature publishing platforms with large communities. With Astro, both are used headless: writers work in their own admin and Astro reads the content by API. Ghost also has newsletters, memberships and paid subscriptions built in, which Markstage does not. Prismic is a hosted page builder: nothing to run, and your developers build the sections. Markstage differs from all three in where writers work (on the Astro site's own design) and where content lives (files in your git repository).

Astro gives content collections (Markdown, MDX, JSON and YAML with a schema) but no editor of its own; its docs send you to a CMS. Markstage is that editor for teams who want writers to work on the site's real design, every save to change only what was edited (with the Markstage server, a commit by the person who saved), and staging, roles, single sign-on and a forms inbox included, all on their own server for free. CloudCannon is the closest hosted product: also git-based, with editing on a preview of the built site, from $55 a month. TinaCMS gives click-to-edit on Astro pages and has edited MDX for longer; Markstage's MDX support is new. Decap CMS needs no server at all and Keystatic runs inside your Astro site, but with both, writers fill in forms rather than editing the page itself. Markstage is pre-release; most products here have years of use and bigger communities.

Where others lead (honestly)

  • MDX. Markstage edits MDX files' words and simple component settings and keeps the code as written, but it shows components as cards, not rendered, and its MDX support is new (docs/MDX.md). TinaCMS, Keystatic and Outstatic have edited MDX for longer.
  • No server at all. Decap CMS and Sveltia CMS run in the browser against your git host. Markstage needs Node.js for a team (sign-in, publishing, the inbox); alone on your computer it runs inside astro dev.
  • A hosted service. Prismic, Ghost(Pro), CloudCannon, TinaCloud and Storyblok run it for you. Markstage is self-hosted only.
  • Publishing and memberships. Ghost has newsletters and paid subscriptions built in; WordPress has a very large ecosystem of themes and plugins.
  • Years of use. Most of these products have been in production for years, with bigger communities and plugin ecosystems. Markstage is pre-release.
  • Frameworks other than Astro. Markstage's core has no framework in it, but it has one adapter so far, Astro; more are planned (see the Roadmap).
