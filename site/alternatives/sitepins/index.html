Sitepins puts a clean editor on top of your repository: it reads your Markdown and front matter, builds the editing screens for you, and commits every save. It says plainly that it is "not a page builder": it edits content inside your layouts. Markstage edits on the rendered page itself.

Who each one is for

Sitepins suits agencies and freelancers handing Astro, Hugo or Next.js sites (including ones made with AI builders) to clients, with a hosted cloud or self-hosting.

Markstage suits teams whose writers should work on an Astro site's real design, with staging, custom roles, single sign-on and a forms inbox. Markstage is pre-release.

Where Sitepins is stronger

  • No schema to write. It reads your files and builds the editor.
  • Live collaboration. Cursors and avatars show who is working where.
  • Agencies. A workspace per client, and a hosted cloud with lifetime pricing.
  • Mobile. Its editor works on phones and tablets.

Moving from Sitepins to Markstage

Your content is already plain files in your repo. List the folders as collections in markstage.config.mjs, add the @markstage/astro integration, and invite your editors to Markstage.

Frequently asked questions

Can I self-host Sitepins?

Yes. Its GitHub repository is under AGPL-3.0 and has self-hosting steps; a hosted cloud is also offered.

Does Sitepins work with Astro?

Yes. Astro's docs have a Sitepins guide.

Does Sitepins edit on the page?

It describes itself as a WYSIWYG editor over your files that does not change design or page structure. Markstage shows the site's own components and CSS while you edit.
